Total runout: the indicator walks the whole length

What it controls

Total runout (⟿) extends circular runout from a single slice to the entire feature length. The indicator stays in contact while the part rotates and travels axially along the datum axis. The tolerance value is the total swing across both motions — radius range over the whole surface.

This is what catches taper, conicity, bow, and overall coaxiality that circular runout misses. Where circular runout = "is this slice round and on-center?", total runout = "is the entire cylindrical surface running true?"

Look at the diagram

The same shaft from the previous lesson, but the indicator now sweeps along the full length while the part rotates. The actual profile shows a slight taper plus a bowed centerline — invisible to circular runout (each slice could be perfect on its own) but caught by total runout because the indicator never resets.

Below, the FCF (⟿ 0.2 A) is shown alongside a "what each control catches" matrix: circular runout sees roundness + center-offset at a slice; total runout adds taper + bow + straightness errors along the axis.

Common mistake

Specifying total runout when only the bearing seat at one slice matters — over-toleranced parts cost real money. The reverse is just as common: putting circular runout on a long bearing journal that mates over its full length, then being surprised when the assembly binds despite passing inspection. Reach for the control that matches the length of the functional contact: spot contact → circular; line contact → total.
