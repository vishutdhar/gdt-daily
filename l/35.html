Projected tolerance zone: push the zone above the part

What it controls

A normal positional tolerance zone for a hole lives inside the part — it bounds the actual hole axis as you can measure it on the part itself. But for a threaded hole that accepts a stud, what really matters isn't where the hole's axis is — it's where the stud's axis ends up after it's threaded in and sticks out above the surface. A small angular wiggle in the hole becomes a big lateral offset 25 mm up the stud.

Projected tolerance zone (Ⓟ) solves this by sliding the cylindrical zone out of the part by a specified height — usually the maximum mating part thickness or stud projection. The zone now bounds the stud where it will actually touch its mate.

Look at the diagram

A plate with a threaded hole, drawn twice. Left: standard position zone — cylinder inside the plate. The hole is tipped slightly; the stud projects from it and lands outside the implied mating tolerance because the projection amplifies the angle. Right: same hole, same tip, but the FCF reads ⌖ Ø0.2 Ⓟ 25 A — the cylindrical zone is projected 25 mm above the surface. Now the stud has to land inside the zone where the mate sits, which forces the hole to be drilled straighter.

A side note shows the FCF syntax: tolerance value, Ⓟ modifier, then the projection height.

Common mistake

Forgetting Ⓟ on threaded holes and discovering at assembly that the studs won't all sit in their through-holes. The hole positions pass inspection on the part by itself; the studs don't fit the mating part. Reach for Ⓟ whenever a fastener projects through and is constrained by another part — particularly tall studs, dowels in deep counterbores, and inserts into thick mating parts. The projection height should equal the maximum mating part thickness above the hole.
