Customized datum reference frame: relax one axis on purpose

What it controls

Sometimes the full six-DOF lock from a three-datum frame is too restrictive. A part might be free to slide along one direction (a slot, a sliding fit) and trying to fully constrain it produces inspection failures that mean nothing functionally. ASME Y14.5-2018 provides the translation modifier (the ▷ symbol, often rendered as Ⓣ in GD&T fonts) placed on the datum letter inside the FCF to allow translation along that datum.

Apply it sparingly. The unlock means the inspector lets the part slide to a functional best fit before measuring; it is not a generic permission to relax rotation.

Look at the diagram

A bracket with a primary datum A (bottom), a secondary datum B (back wall — but it's a slot, so the part can slide in X), and a tertiary C. Without the modifier, the FCF reads ⌖ Ø0.2 A B C — and inspection requires datum B to fully arrest X-translation, which fails on this slotted feature. With the modifier, the FCF reads ⌖ Ø0.2 A B Ⓣ C — the inspector aligns to B but lets the part slide along X to best-fit before measuring.

A small inset shows where the translation modifier sits (immediately to the right of the datum letter inside the FCF).

Common mistake

Reaching for the translation modifier as a way to "make the part pass." The unlock is a functional declaration: it says "this part is supposed to be free in this direction at this datum." If you add Ⓣ just because parts fail inspection without it, you've made the drawing lie about what the design actually needs. The right test: when this part is installed in the assembly, can it really move along that direction? If yes, the modifier is correct. If no, the parts are bad — keep the datum locked.
